I’m Rabuor Tindi - friends call me Tindi - and I’m a student studying Chemical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Alabama. Roll Tide.

This is the honest story of why I built Scout.

## Hundreds of applications, the same loop

During my first real internship hunt, I did what every engineering student is told to do: apply early, apply often, and treat volume like a strategy.

So I did.

I sent **hundreds of engineering internship applications**. I tailored resumes at midnight. I rebuilt cover letters that asked the same questions in slightly different words. I logged into Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, and USAJOBS portals until the browser tabs blurred together.

And the responses that came back were mostly the same: rejection, silence, or a polite “we’ve moved forward with other candidates.”

I wasn’t angry that I didn’t land every role. Competition is real. What wore me down was how **repetitive, time-consuming, and inefficient** the process felt. The busywork wasn’t making me a better candidate - it was stealing hours I could have spent preparing for interviews, shipping projects, or actually talking to people.

Somewhere between another autofilled “Are you authorized to work…” form and another “upload your resume again (as PDF)” prompt, I realized the internship hunt had turned into unpaid data entry.

## The question that became Scout

If so much of the process is mechanical, why are students still doing it by hand?

That question became Scout.

Scout is an **AI job application agent** - not a spam blaster, and not a brittle “auto apply” script that dumps the same resume into every field. It’s built around a clearer loop:

1. Understand your resume and experience
2. Match you to roles that actually fit
3. Handle **internship application automation** with care - including fallbacks when a portal fights back
4. Give you visibility into what happened so you stay in control

In other words: **automated job applications** that feel closer to a junior teammate working with you than a chrome extension spraying applications into the void.

## Building while being a student

I’m building Scout alongside classes, labs, exams, and everything else that comes with being a student. That constraint isn’t a cute footnote - it’s the point.

Students don’t need another productivity lecture. We need **AI career tools for students** that respect limited time and still take the hunt seriously. Scout has to earn trust while I’m sitting in the same system it’s trying to fix.

Some weeks progress is sharp. Some weeks Browserbase sessions fight me harder than thermodynamics. Both are normal. Shipping Scout in public means admitting that the agent is still improving - and that “beta” is a promise that we iterate, not a disclaimer that quality doesn’t matter.

## What Scout’s mission is

Scout’s mission is simple:

**Help students spend less time filling forms and more time preparing for opportunities.**

That means:

- Less grinding through identical application portals
- Better signal on which roles are worth your attention
- Clear explanations when something can’t be completed automatically
- A product built by someone who still lives the student experience, not a vague HR platform bolted onto “AI”

I’m not trying to remove ambition from the job search. I’m trying to remove the waste layered on top of it.

## Why this story matters now

Scout is in beta because this problem is too large to pretend we’ve finished it. Browser automation breaks. ATS portals change. Edge cases exist that only show up at 1 a.m. when a student is counting on you.

But the direction is fixed.

If you’ve ever stared at a board full of open roles and felt your energy drain before you even started applying, you already understand why Scout exists. If you’ve watched friends with strong resumes lose weeks to forms instead of interviews, you understand it even more.

I built Scout because I lived that loop - and I refused to accept that **engineering internship applications** had to stay that painful forever.

If you’re here early: thank you. Follow along as we harden the agent, widen ATS coverage, and keep aiming for a future where students compete on substance - not on who can tolerate more busywork.

Never apply again is the ambition.

Building the AI application agent that gets us there - week by week - is the work.

- Tindi
